# National (Cross-Regional) Student Housing Search Pain Points and Tool Landscape

Research date: 2026-11-19. Scope: US colleges, evidence preferred 2022-2026. Region-specific differences are intentionally excluded.

Method note: web search tools rarely returned Reddit threads directly (searches with "reddit" returned mostly university pages, campus newspapers, and guides). Student voices below therefore come mostly from campus newspapers quoting students and from forums. Where no Reddit thread URL could be verified, nothing was attributed to Reddit.

## 1. When to start: early lease-signing pressure

### Takeaway
At many college towns, off-campus leases are signed 9-12 months ahead, often in the fall of the prior year. Students describe this as peer-driven panic that forces them to commit before they know their roommates. Others report that landlords cut prices in spring to fill units that are still empty.

### Cited Findings
- East Lansing students typically begin searching a full year before move-in. A community liaison says peers drive the pressure and that an October housing fair intensifies it (2022) — [The State News, Nov 2022](https://statenews.com/article/2022/11/year-out-lease-signing-in-east-lansing-causes-stress-among-students)
- Student paraphrase: by October you have met people but don't know them well enough to commit to living with them for a year — [The State News, Nov 2022](https://statenews.com/article/2022/11/year-out-lease-signing-in-east-lansing-causes-stress-among-students)
- A 2026 student opinion piece argues that early lease signing damages friendships, because groups form, or break apart, around housing decisions made months in advance — [The State News, Feb 2026](https://statenews.com/article/2026/02/opinion-early-lease-signing-is-detrimental-to-friendships-housing-guide)
- Northwestern students report feeling pressure to sign 6-12 months before moving in — [North by Northwestern](https://northbynorthwestern.com/housing-hustle)
- Miami University (Ohio) students called the search very stressful, very difficult and expensive. They said per-semester pricing makes places feel out of reach (Mar 2023) — [The Miami Student, Mar 2023](https://miamistudent.net/article/2023/03/very-stressful-very-difficult-and-expensive-navigating-the-oxford-housing-market-as-a-student)
- An upperclassman's counterpoint: landlords often cut prices sharply in spring to fill vacancies, so rushing is not always rational (2019, older) — [Miami University Student Life](https://sites.miamioh.edu/student-life/2019/11/advice-from-an-upperclassman/); see also [Onward State, 2019](https://onwardstate.com/2019/10/02/theres-no-need-to-rush-into-securing-off-campus-housing/)
- Baylor seniors' advice for off-campus living (Jan 2026) — [Baylor Lariat, Jan 2026](https://baylorlariat.com/2026/01/26/seniors-offer-advice-for-off-campus-living/?amp=1)

### Inferences
- The core problem is an information asymmetry about timing. Students can't tell whether the local market truly requires early signing or whether the urgency is FOMO that landlords amplify. A tool showing local signing-timeline norms and vacancy and price trends by month would address this directly.
- Early signing also pushes the roommate decision earlier, which feeds the roommate failures described in section 5.

### Gaps
- No national survey quantifying when students sign leases was found.

## 2. Finding listings and scams

### Takeaway
Rental scams fall disproportionately on young adults. In FTC data, people ages 18-29 are three times more likely than other adults to report losing money to a rental scam. About half of reported rental scams start with a fake Facebook ad, and the FTC explicitly names college sublet and housing Facebook groups as a hunting ground. Universities nationwide now post recurring scam alerts.

### Cited Findings
- FTC Data Spotlight (Dec 22, 2025) covering Jan 2020-Jun 2025:
  - nearly 65,000 rental scam reports and about $65M in reported losses, with a median loss of $1,000. The FTC says this likely understates actual harm.
  - In Jul 2024-Jun 2025, people 18-29 were 3x more likely than other adults to report a monetary loss.
  - Share of reports with a monetary loss, by age: 18-29 was 46%, compared with 23% for 30-39 and 13% for 40-49.
  - For the 12 months ending Jun 2025, about half of rental scam reports started with a fake Facebook ad, and 16% started with a fake Craigslist listing.
  - Tactics: cloned real listings with changed contact info, below-market rent, upfront application fees, deposits or first month's rent before a viewing, "$1 credit check" sign-ups that enroll victims in paid memberships, identity theft through applications asking for SSN, license photos and pay stubs, and fake self-guided tours with lockbox codes.
  - The FTC notes that young adults are targeted in Facebook groups college students use for sublets and near-campus housing.
  - Source: [FTC, Dec 2025](https://ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/12/rental-scams-hit-home-65-million-reported-losses); [PDF](https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/rental-scams-spotlight-2025.pdf)
- University of Illinois police (Mar 2024): a student answered a Facebook sublease ad and paid $550 for an "application fee and security deposit" to a scammer — [UIUC Police](https://police.illinois.edu/fake-apartment-sublease/)
- University of Michigan DPSS warned of subletting scams in off-campus housing Facebook groups — [ClickOnDetroit](https://www.clickondetroit.com/topic/University_of_Michigan_Division_of_Public_Safety_&_Security/)
- Missouri S&T (Aug 2024) warned students about listings on Facebook Marketplace — [Missouri S&T](https://econnection.mst.edu/2024/08/beware-of-housing-scams/)
- Massachusetts consumer office (Aug 2025): scammers copy photos from real listings, advertise them at a lower price, and pressure students to sign quickly — [Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/news/get-schooled-on-rental-scams)
- Local news in 2026 shows the problem is ongoing:
  - [Atlanta News First, Aug 2026](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/08/05/rental-scammers-are-targeting-college-students-heres-how-spot-fake-listing/?outputType=amp)
  - [Deseret News, Jun 2026, Facebook housing scams](https://www.deseret.com/utah/2026/06/30/facbook-housing-scams/)
  - [KSL, "The housing hustle: students, summers and scam artists"](https://www.ksl.com/article/news/features/education/the-housing-hustle-students-summers-and-scam-artists/51583368)
  - [WPTV, students targeted in off-campus subletting scams](https://www.wptv.com/money/consumer/college-students-targeted-in-off-campus-subletting-scams)
  - [WCBI, fake rental and subleasing ads](https://www.wcbi.com/?p=2998823)
- University of Michigan's sublet guide warns of a common scam involving a check that won't clear — [UMich Student Life](https://studentlife.umich.edu/parents/article/plan-now-summer-subletting)

### Inferences
- The channels students rely on most (Facebook groups and Marketplace, Craigslist, school GroupMe and Discord) have no identity verification and no proof of ownership or lease authority. That gap is exactly where the losses happen. Two features could close it: verified listers, such as a .edu-verified subletter or a landlord verified against property records, and escrowed deposits.
- Students who search remotely, including incoming freshmen, transfers and international students, can't do the in-person viewing that every scam guide recommends. That makes them structurally more exposed.

### Gaps
- No FTC figure isolates students specifically; the 18-29 age band is the closest proxy.

## 3. Evaluating listings and landlords

### Takeaway
Little verified data was found. Students lack reliable reviews of landlords and property managers. Mainstream listing sites show rent but not the total cost, such as per-semester pricing, utilities and fees.

### Cited Findings
- Per-semester or per-bed pricing makes the true cost hard to compare (Miami University students, 2023) — [The Miami Student](https://miamistudent.net/article/2023/03/very-stressful-very-difficult-and-expensive-navigating-the-oxford-housing-market-as-a-student)

### Fees and deposits (hidden costs at application and signing)
- Rental application fees range from $25 to $350. Fees stack when students apply to several places in a tight market. Some states cap them, for example New York at $20 — [NPR, Jan 2023](https://www.npr.org/2023/01/13/1148426491/rental-application-fees-housing-affordable-market-states-laws); [NCLC Junk Fees report](https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/JunkFees-Rpt.pdf)
- UNT student research in Denton, TX found that junk fees add about 5% to renters' bills. Students were surprised by charges billed on top of rent (Jan 2025) — [KERA, Jan 2025](https://www.keranews.org/news/2025-01-27/junk-fees-add-5-to-denton-renters-bills-new-unt-student-research-shows)
- Ann Arbor property managers charged "waitlist" fees of up to about $6-7K to reserve 2024-25 apartments. A tenants' union says fees get renamed to dodge local bans — [Michigan Daily](https://www.michigandaily.com/news/ann-arbor/ann-arbor-landlords-continue-to-charge-waitlist-fees-to-prospective-tenants/); [CBS Detroit](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/detroit/news/ann-arbor-renters-charged-6k-in-waitlist-fees-for-apartments)
- Boston: landlords and brokers illegally charged student renters fees — [Daily Free Press](https://dailyfreepress.com/04/29/03/178893/student-renters-charged-illegal-fees-face-tenancy-violations/)
- The FTC's final junk-fee rule (late 2024) does not cover rental housing, according to NCLC — [NCLC 2025 exec summary](https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/202511_Report_What-the-Heck-Dude_Exec-Summary.pdf)
- Utah Valley students report hidden student housing fees — [UVU Review](https://www.uvureview.com/news/hidden-student-housing-fees-2/)

### Gaps
- No verified national data was found on students' access to landlord reviews. Aggregated student complaints about listing accuracy on Zillow and Apartments.com were also not found, beyond scammers cloning those sites' listings (section 8).
- Remote touring: no dedicated student-voiced source was found. Scam guides universally advise in-person viewing, which remote students can't do.

## 4. Applications, guarantors, and credit checks (international and first-time renters)

### Takeaway
Students with no US credit history are routinely asked for a guarantor or co-signer, who usually must be a US citizen or permanent resident with an SSN and documented income. This includes nearly all international students and many 18-19-year-old first-time renters. The fallbacks are costly: a deposit of 2-3 months' rent, or a paid guarantor service.

### Cited Findings
- Landlords often request a guarantor or co-signer when there's no US credit history — [CSU Fullerton](https://international.fullerton.edu/prospective-students/additional-information/housing/off-campus-housing.html)
- A guarantor must be a creditworthy US citizen or permanent resident with a bank statement or pay stub — [BU Medical Campus OHR](https://www.bumc.bu.edu/ohr/internationalstudents)
- Most landlords want the guarantor to have a US SSN — [JMU ISSS](https://www.jmu.edu/global/isss/admitted/gradhousing.shtml)
- Without a guarantor, international students may be required to pay a security deposit of 2 months' rent — [JHU (PDF, 2023)](https://me.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/International-Students.pdf)
- Many landlords accept 2-3 months' rent as a deposit in place of a guarantor or credit check — [f1jobs guide](https://www.f1jobs.io/resources/blog/renting-apartment-no-us-credit-international)
- Paid guarantor services are an option for students without a qualifying US guarantor. Fees and eligibility vary — [Amber guide](https://amberstudent.com/blog/us-apartment-guarantors-for-international-students-requirements-costs-and-alternatives)
- NYC: international students struggle to find a guarantor. A housing expert quoted in the piece argues that charging these students larger deposits may be illegal. This is a single source and the legality varies by jurisdiction — [City Limits](https://citylimits.org/international-students-in-nyc-face-apartment-hunt-hurdle-finding-a-guarantor/)
- Older forum evidence: students without credit history report landlords rejecting them outright (2012, dated) — [GradCafe](https://forum.thegradcafe.com/topic/33871-renting-an-apartment-with-no-credit-history-in-the-us)
- More university guides on international renting:
  - [UC Denver](https://ucdenver.edu/housing-and-dining/off-campus-housing/international-student-resources)
  - [UT Dallas](https://icp.utdallas.edu/resources/housing/apartment-resource)
  - [Redfin international student guide](https://www.redfin.com/blog/international-student-renting-guide/)

### Inferences
- The guarantor requirement compounds with remote touring and scam exposure: international students must commit sight-unseen, in a market they don't know, while also posting extra cash.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on the share of international students denied leases was found.

## 5. Roommates: matching, ghosting, and joint-and-several liability

### Takeaway
Most group student leases are joint and several. When one roommate leaves or stops paying, the landlord can pursue any remaining tenant for the full rent. Courts generally enforce these clauses, and the only recourse is suing the departed roommate in small claims court.

### Cited Findings
- Under joint-and-several leases, every signer remains liable for full rent until the lease is formally amended, and most courts enforce such clauses — [RentCafe](https://www.rentcafe.com/blog/daily-living/what-happens-when-roommates-move-out-early/); [Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/when-roommate-moves-out-29520.html)
- Remaining tenants can pursue the departed roommate in small claims. A roommate agreement can't override the lease but supports such a claim — [RentCafe](https://www.rentcafe.com/blog/daily-living/roommate-stops-paying-rent-can-you-be-evicted/)
- The UCLA Student Legal Services article on roommate issues — [UCLA SLS (PDF)](https://studentlegal.ucla.edu/assets/pdf/articles/roommatearticle6_15.pdf)
- GWU's off-campus office guidance on roommates — [GWU Off-Campus](https://offcampus.students.gwu.edu/node/90)
- Group houses debate whether joint and several liability is fair (2017, older) — [PoPville](https://www.popville.com/2017/03/joint-several-liability-group-house-is-this-fair/)

### Inferences
- Early signing (section 1), immature roommate commitments, and joint-and-several liability combine into a common failure mode. A roommate drops out or leaves mid-year, and the remaining students cover the rent or scramble for a replacement.
- Individual-lease, by-the-bed purpose-built student housing exists partly to sell protection from this risk, often at a premium.

### Gaps
- No survey data on how often student roommate arrangements fail.

## 6. Subleasing (summer internships, study abroad)

### Takeaway
The 12-month lease cycle clashes with the 9-month academic year, internships and study abroad. Students often sublet at a loss and rely on personal networks. Landlord approval requirements add friction, and the sublet market is the most scam-prone segment.

### Cited Findings
- A Penn student cut sublet rent from $900 to $550 to attract a subletter. Most students subletting summer housing don't recover their full rent (older, 2007 and 2016) — [Daily Pennsylvanian 2007](https://www.thedp.com/article/2007/03/with_leases_signed_students_hunt_for_subletters); [Daily Pennsylvanian 2016](https://www.thedp.com/article/2016/04/off-campus-subletter-difficulties)
- Student paraphrase: it's only easy to find a subletter if you already know people — [Daily Pennsylvanian](https://www.thedp.com/article/2016/04/off-campus-subletter-difficulties)
- Many landlords require subtenants to pass the same approval process as tenants, and a written sublease is needed to set out dates and damage responsibility — [UMich Student Life](https://studentlife.umich.edu/parents/article/plan-now-summer-subletting)
- UVA in 2025 shows how lopsided sublet supply and demand can be. Leases run 12 months, and spring study abroad far outnumbers fall (476 vs. 245 students in 2023-24), so students going abroad in spring compete for few takers.
  - One student going abroad in spring started looking in early fall and still found no subtenant.
  - Another posted in a roughly 130-member "U.Va. Housing" GroupMe and got 6 replies in a day.
  - An intern turned down a friend's purpose-built room at about $1,200/month and sublet a friend's house room at about $500.
  - One sublet turned out to violate the original lease. The lease had to be formally transferred to the subtenant about a week before move-in.
  - A student said he wished he had known earlier that the Education Abroad office could connect him with incoming exchange students.
  - Source: [Cavalier Daily, Jun 2025](https://www.cavalierdaily.com/article/2025/06/students-share-their-experiences-with-the-subleasing-process-in-charlottesville)
- Some schools run free sublet listings with housing-office approval, for example Johns Hopkins — [JHU (PDF)](https://studentaffairs.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2017/01/sublet-applications.pdf)
- Sublet listings in Facebook groups are a primary FTC-flagged scam vector (section 2) — [FTC, Dec 2025](https://ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/12/rental-scams-hit-home-65-million-reported-losses)

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on how many students sublet or on average sublet discounts.

## 7. Housing insecurity: macro data

### Takeaway
Nearly half of surveyed students experience housing insecurity, and about 1 in 7 experience homelessness. Few use the assistance available to them.

### Cited Findings
- Hope Center 2023-2024 Student Basic Needs Survey. It covered 74,350 students at 91 institutions in 16 states, was fielded Spring 2023-Summer 2024 and published Feb 2025.
  - 48% of students were housing insecure and 14% experienced homelessness.
  - 59% experienced at least one basic-needs insecurity.
  - Only 12% of housing-insecure or homeless students used public housing or utility assistance.
  - Nearly 75% of Black and Indigenous students reported food and/or housing insecurity, compared with 55% of White students.
  - Source: [Hope Center](https://hope.temple.edu/2023-2024-student-basic-needs-survey-report); [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/send-help-59-of-students-face-insecurities-around-needs-like-food-and/741774/)
- Caveat: the sample skews toward partner institutions, which include many community colleges. It is not a nationally representative sample. For federal longitudinal data (NPSAS), see [Hope Center NPSAS](https://hope.temple.edu/npsas).
- Portland State 2023 survey: 19.1% of students reported homelessness and 54.1% housing insecurity — [PSU](https://www.pdx.edu/homelessness/homelessness-psu)
- Signs of an on-campus bed shortage, which pushes students off campus earlier:
  - UNC Chapel Hill had about 1,300 students on the 2025-26 housing waitlist, up from about 1,100 — [Daily Tar Heel, Jan 2025](https://dailytarheel.com/article/2025/01/university-housing-waitlist-exploration)
  - At UConn, enrollment growth is straining housing. The cheapest on-campus option is $8,066/yr, and off-campus options can cost more than twice that — [CT Public, Oct 2025](https://www.ctpublic.org/2025-10-24/uconn-student-housing-shortage)
  - Columbia College Chicago ran out of on-campus housing for returning students — [Columbia Chronicle](https://columbiachronicle.com/campus/breaking-columbia-runs-out-of-on-campus-housing-for-returning-students/)
  - At the University of Utah, the on-campus waitlist once reached about 3,500, and schools used hotels and incentives to cope (undated, so treat with caution) — [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/51155)
  - These are cited only as examples of a nationwide pattern. Regional detail belongs to other researchers.

## 9. Move-out and deposit return

### Takeaway
Deposit deductions for cleaning, paint and carpet are a recurring fall dispute in college towns. Students say landlords count on them not knowing their rights. No national US statistic exists. UK surveys suggest that 38-49% of students don't get their full deposit back.

### Cited Findings
- University of Illinois, Oct 2024:
  - Students at several Champaign-Urbana companies were charged $500-$750 for cleaning, painting and carpet. They say the units were clean and that they got no receipts.
  - Dispute emails went unanswered, and one dispute was "lost" by the company.
  - A small landlord asked for Venmo, then went silent until Student Legal Services intervened.
  - SLS was booked about 2 weeks out in peak season. Students believe landlords rely on student ignorance.
  - SLS advice: document the unit's condition at move-in and move-out and keep records 30-60 days.
  - Source: [Daily Illini, Oct 2024](https://dailyillini.com/special-sections/housing-guide/2024/10/10/students-take-action-against-withheld-security-deposits/)
- UK 2018 Nationwide poll of 1,003 former students: 49% didn't get their full deposit back, and 79% of those said it was withheld unjustly. This is UK data, offered as the only quantitative proxy found — [Nationwide](https://www.nationwide.co.uk/media/news/half-of-students-lose-rental-deposit-with-eight-in-ten-saying-money-held-unfairly)
- A BYU official estimated that fewer than 1% of off-campus students had deposit problems per year. This is an anecdotal estimate from 2017 and conflicts with the UK and Illinois evidence — [BYU Universe, 2017](https://universe.byu.edu/2017/06/01/security-deposits1)

### Gaps
- No national US survey of student deposit losses was found.

## 8. Tools students use and their shortcomings

### Takeaway
The de facto student housing "platform" is a patchwork of informal social channels: school Facebook groups, GroupMe, Instagram "Class of" pages, Discord, and friends of friends. These are free and dense with students, but they are unverified and are the top FTC scam vector. Mainstream portals like Zillow and Apartments.com aren't built for by-the-bed, academic-year, sublet or roommate needs, and scammers clone their listings onto Facebook. Student-specific platforms such as Places4Students, Off-Campus Partners, Kopa, sublet apps and university portals are fragmented, school-by-school, and thinly reviewed.

### Cited Findings
- **Facebook groups and Marketplace**
  - Facebook groups are the main sublet channel. Some groups require admin approval just to see posts — [The Ithacan](https://theithacan.org/?p=46234)
  - The UNC Chapel Hill subleasing group had about 33.5K members (2023 satire piece) — [Daily Tar Heel, Oct 2023](https://www.dailytarheel.com/article/2023/10/opinion-satire-facebook-groups-housing)
  - Scammers post fake subleases in unofficial university Facebook groups and on Craigslist — [Minnesota Daily](https://mndaily.com/217186/city/ctapartmentscam)
  - About 50% of rental scams start with a fake Facebook ad — [FTC, Dec 2025](https://ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/12/rental-scams-hit-home-65-million-reported-losses)
  - Boston renters post in Facebook groups but move conversations to Instagram DMs — [Boston Globe, Aug 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/26/real-estate/move-in-day-boston-september-1-facebook-gen-z/)
- **GroupMe and Instagram**
  - GroupMe works when a group is active, but results are hit-or-miss: one spring study-abroad student found nobody — [Cavalier Daily, Jun 2025](https://www.cavalierdaily.com/article/2025/06/students-share-their-experiences-with-the-subleasing-process-in-charlottesville)
  - Instagram "Class of" pages are used for roommate searches. Some such groups are unaffiliated with the school and charge fees — [Her Campus](https://www.hercampus.com/?p=1690787); [Queen's OCLA](https://www.queensu.ca/ocla/looking-housemates-search)
- **Zillow and Apartments.com**
  - Scammers clone active Zillow and Apartments.com listings (photos, address, description) and repost them cheaper elsewhere — [Security Hero](https://www.securityhero.io/scam-database/rental-listing-fraud/); [SpotEasy MIT guide](https://www.spoteasy.com/blog/how-can-mit-students-spot-fake-rental-listings-around-campus-before-its-too-late)
  - Apartments.com offers a verified-listing badge, and Zillow says it screens most fake listings before they go live. These claims come from secondary sources, and no student complaints specific to these platforms were found.
- **University off-campus portals**
  - Duke runs an off-campus housing portal with scam guidance — [Duke Off-Campus Housing](https://offcampushousing.students.duke.edu/avoid-scams-and-fraud)
  - UVA's Education Abroad office points students to U-loop for sublets — [Cavalier Daily](https://www.cavalierdaily.com/article/2025/06/students-share-their-experiences-with-the-subleasing-process-in-charlottesville)
  - Johns Hopkins runs an approval-based sublet program — [JHU PDF](https://studentaffairs.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2017/01/sublet-applications.pdf)
- **Places4Students**
  - Free, active since 2003, with listings for rentals, roommates and sublets. This description comes from a promotional source — [badcredit.org](https://www.badcredit.org/news/housing-help-for-students-landlords-and-educational-institutions/)
  - Its App Store listing has very few reviews, one of them complaining that the user couldn't find their school — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id931562882)
- **Kopa**
  - Kopa offers mid-term and student rentals. Reviews are mixed: a 2022 one-star review cites no customer support and fees — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/kopa.co)
  - A Product Hunt reviewer reports a deposit that was never returned, though the sample is only 4 reviews — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/kopa/reviews)
- **New student-built sublet apps keep appearing**, a sign of unmet demand but also more fragmentation:
  - [Sublyst](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sublyst/id6749684934)
  - [Subletsy](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/subletsy/id6746137940)
  - [Let's Sub](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lets-sub-sublets-with-a-swipe/id1110749892)
  - [RoomMe](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/roomme-by-dsb/id1615821899)
- **Student.com** has a one-star scam allegation, which the developer disputes — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/student-com-find-your-home/id1288515333?uo=4)

### Inferences
- Pain points cut across tools. No single tool combines:
  1. student-verified identity, through .edu login
  2. verified landlord or lease authority
  3. academic-calendar lease terms and sublet matching
  4. roommate matching tied to lease liability
  5. deposit and fee transparency
  6. support for remote students, international students and students without a guarantor
- Network effects keep students on Facebook and GroupMe despite the scam risk. A new tool would need to win on trust and verification while seeding liquidity per campus. One route is partnering with housing and study-abroad offices, which already broker sublets informally, as UVA's Education Abroad office does.

### Gaps
- I couldn't retrieve Reddit threads (r/college, r/StudentHousing, r/Renters, r/legaladvice) through search, so no Reddit-sourced quotes or URLs are included. Direct Reddit searching is recommended.
- College Confidential, Niche and Unigo threads were not surfaced.
- No reviews or market-share data were found for Off-Campus Partners, Roomies, SpareRoom or Leasebreak, and nothing on how students use Discord for housing.

## 10. Specific groups (cross-cutting summary)

### Takeaway
Each stage is hardest for students who are remote, lack credit, or lack local networks.

### Cited Findings
- **International students:** they need a guarantor, a 2-3-month deposit or a paid guarantor service, and they usually tour remotely, which leaves them scam-exposed.
